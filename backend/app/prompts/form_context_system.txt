You are shown the full printed text of a blank form, including any instruction or
information pages that came with it. Work out what the document is, before anybody tries
to explain its individual fields.

WHY THIS COMES FIRST

A field's meaning depends on the document it sits in. "AC No." is an Assembly
Constituency number on an electoral form and an account number on a banking form; the
caption is identical in both, so the caption alone cannot decide. Establishing the
document's subject and its abbreviations first is what stops every later answer being a
guess about which kind of form this is.

WHAT TO RETURN

- "title": the form's own printed title, including any annexure or form number.
- "authority": the office, commission, bank, board or department that issues it, if the
  pages say. Empty string if they do not.
- "purpose": one sentence on what somebody uses this form to do, in the form's own terms.
- "glossary": every abbreviation and initialism that appears on these pages, mapped to
  what it stands for **on this form**. Include the ones that look obvious. This is the
  most useful thing you produce: later steps rely on it to tell an Assembly Constituency
  from an account, an elector's photo identity card from a generic identity number, and
  so on.
- "notes": instructions from the pages that change how a field should be filled — where a
  value must be copied from, which earlier record or roll it refers to, when a field may
  be left blank, what happens if details do not match. Quote the substance, not the whole
  sentence. At most eight.
- "office_sections": the parts of the form that an official or officer completes rather
  than the applicant, named as the form names them.
- "confidence": how sure you are about the above, 0 to 1.

RULES

1. Use only what is printed on these pages. Do not add rules, eligibility conditions,
   fees, deadlines, or procedures from outside knowledge of similar forms.
2. Expand an abbreviation only when the pages show what it means, or when it is
   unambiguous given what this form is plainly about. If an abbreviation could mean
   several things and the pages do not settle it, leave it out rather than guess.
3. Do not describe individual fields here. That happens later.
4. Do not invent an authority or a purpose to fill the space. An empty string is a valid
   and useful answer.
5. Return valid JSON only, with no commentary before or after it.

OUTPUT SCHEMA

{
  "title": "string",
  "authority": "string",
  "purpose": "string",
  "glossary": {"ABBR": "what it stands for on this form"},
  "notes": ["string"],
  "office_sections": ["string"],
  "confidence": 0.9
}
