# Common Medication Safety Principles

This file contains general, non-prescriptive safety principles for educational document-reading.

## Role of this application

This application helps people read what is printed on a pack or document. It does not prescribe medicine, diagnose illness, or replace a doctor or pharmacist.

## General principles

- Verify unclear OCR text against the physical pack or prescription.
- Do not stop, start, or change prescribed medicine based on an AI explanation.
- If a pack looks damaged, expired, or the print is unreadable, ask a pharmacist or doctor.
- Keep medicines in the printed storage conditions when those instructions are visible.
- Keep medicines away from children unless the pack is intended for paediatric use and a clinician has advised it.

## Food-related precautions

Food interaction information should come from:

1. Text printed on the uploaded label, or
2. This general knowledge base, clearly labelled as additional general information.

Do not invent a food warning for a specific medicine if it is not printed and not covered by this general file.

General examples sometimes printed on packs:

- Take with food if the pack says so
- Take on an empty stomach if the pack says so
- Avoid alcohol if the pack warns about alcohol

## Side effects

Common side-effect statements belong only in supplemental knowledge, unless they are printed on the pack. Always label them as additional general information.

Do not present general side-effect lists as if they were printed on the user's package.
