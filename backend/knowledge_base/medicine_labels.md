# Medicine Label Interpretation

This knowledge file explains how to read printed medicine packaging. It is educational. It is not medical advice.

## What a typical label contains

Medicine packs often print:

- Product or medicine name
- Strength, for example `500 mg`
- Batch or lot number
- Manufacturing date, often marked `MFG` or `MFD`
- Expiry date, often marked `EXP`, `Expiry`, `Use Before`, or `Best Before`
- Dosage text printed on the pack
- Storage instructions
- Manufacturer name
- Warnings

## How to talk about values

Only report values that are visible on the uploaded label.

If a field is missing or OCR is unclear, say that it could not be found confidently.

Never invent an expiry date, batch number, strength, or dosage.

## Strength

Strength usually appears next to the medicine name, such as `500 mg` or `250 mg/5 ml`. Preserve the printed unit exactly.

## Dosage text

Printed dosage text may say "as directed by physician" or a schedule such as "one tablet twice daily". Repeat the printed wording. Do not change a prescribed dose.

## Dates

Expiry and manufacturing dates may use month-year format such as `DEC 2027` or numeric dates. Keep the printed form.
